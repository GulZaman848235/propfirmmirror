Questions

What is a prop firm?

A prop firm, short for proprietary trading firm, gives traders an account to trade under its rules and pays them a share of the profits. Most prop firms sell an evaluation, often called a challenge: reach the profit target without breaking the daily loss or maximum drawdown limit and you receive a funded account. Some sell instant accounts with no evaluation. Many firms state that their funded accounts are simulated, so check how payouts work in each firm's terms.

How do I compare prop firms?

Start with your market (forex and CFDs, futures or crypto), then compare the rules that decide whether you pass and get paid: profit target, daily loss limit, maximum drawdown and its type (static, trailing or end-of-day), consistency rule, minimum trading days, profit split and payout schedule. Then check the price for your account size and whether your country is on the firm's restricted list. The table on this page puts these side by side, and each firm's page links the official source for every rule.

How can I tell if a prop firm is legit?

Judge each firm on evidence rather than marketing: its payout record, what traders report across review platforms, how long it has operated, and whether it publishes its rules, legal entity, head office and restricted countries. The Mirror Score combines these, and each firm's page shows the reviews, payout reports and official sources behind it, with dates. Always read the firm's own terms before you buy.

What is the Mirror Score?

The Mirror Score is Prop Firm Mirror's opinion of a firm, out of 10, given separately in each section the firm sells in. Payout record counts for 35% (payout reports in reviews and independent on-chain payout trackers), public reviews 35% (every review platform, weighted by how many reviews stand behind each), rules and cost 18% (the firm's published rules and prices) and track record 12% (years in business and what the firm discloses). It is not a guarantee of how a firm will treat you.

Where does Prop Firm Mirror get its data?

Rules come only from each firm's own website, help centre, FAQ, terms or official announcements. Review sites, other comparison sites, YouTube, Reddit and Discord screenshots are never used as a source for a rule. Every fact carries its source link and the date it was checked, firms are re-checked daily on a rolling schedule so each one is read again every three days, and anything a firm does not publish is marked as not published.

No. Firms cannot pay to be listed, scored or ranked, the site has no affiliate links, and the order you see comes from the Mirror Score or the sort you choose.
